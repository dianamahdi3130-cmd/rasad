# Khasab, 26-27 Nov 2026: new heat at sea, seen by NASA, not posted

**The owner's question** (27 Nov, early morning): another channel posted a video titled «إستهداف سفينة
مخالفة في مضيق هرمز بالقرب من خصب في عمان». Did Rasad see it, why was it not posted, and is it a new
ship? All times below are Baghdad time.

## What the pictures show

**Meteosat-12, every 10 minutes** (probe tries 44 and 45: `hotspots`, `seascan`, `pictures`):
- **Before:** the earlier fire off Khasab (23-25 Nov, `hormuz_one_source_20261125.md`) was last seen on
  the evening of 25 Nov. After that there was no heat at sea in the box for about 22 hours.
- **26 Nov 22:00:** heat appears at sea at 26.31 N, 56.30 E, about 9 km north of Khasab. It is seen
  until 23:20, is gone from 23:30 to 01:00, and is back from 01:10 to 04:10, moving north-east.
- **27 Nov 02:40-03:40:** the strongest spot within 3 km reads 7.0-7.9 at 02:50, 03:00 and 03:20
  (6.0-8.0 above the clear sea), 2.0 at 03:10 (faded), and 5.1-5.2 at 03:30-03:40. The warming is
  4.5-6.8 km long. The 10.5 micron picture shows no clear change at the spot itself.
- This on-off-on pattern at 02:50-03:30 is what the other channel's video shows.

**NASA, NOAA-20, 27 Nov 01:10:** two night pixels at 26.283 N, 56.252-56.256 E, of 3.18 and 3.7 MW,
nominal confidence.

**Rasad's own records within 25 km:**
- The earlier fire's last record ended on 25 Nov at 22:00.
- There is no record between then and 27 Nov 01:10: no NASA pixel and no finder spot.

## Why it was not posted

1. **NASA's pixels: the earlier ship made the spot "known".**
   - The record `20261126-2210_26.283_56.256` holds `known_days` 1, `area_days` 9 and
     «يحتاج تأكيداً». It is in the app and was not posted.
   - `brain.open_sea_fire` refuses any spot with `known_days` above 0. That one earlier day was the
     burning ship of 23-25 Nov.
   - In open water the rest of the tool calls heat usual only when it burns on most days
     (`engine.usual_heat`, decided on 23 Nov for this same strait). `open_sea_fire` does not follow
     that rule.
   - No other road applied:
     - not «strong»: under 10 MW and not high confidence;
     - no confirmed sea fire within 24 hours: the earlier one ended about 27 hours before.
2. **The night finder saw no sudden rise.**
   - The finder looks for a rise against the pictures 30-90 minutes before.
   - This heat came in slowly and stayed weak.
   - The whole-night replay of the box (18:50Z-01:20Z) gives 0 spots. In every picture all 19-41
     candidates were rejected, mostly as "no warmer than the clear sea" or "a lone pixel at the
     threshold". The log keeps only counts, so the reason for this spot itself is not known.
   - The steady rule (switched off on 25 Nov, `steady_min_above` 8) would not have told it in the hour
     read: the spot was 8 or more in one picture of that hour, not three in a row.

## Reading

- **Most likely a new event, not the earlier ship coming back.** No satellite saw heat there for
  about a day before it.
- **It cannot be ruled out** that the burnt hull flared again: the new heat lies within 5 km of the
  earlier track.
- **Not known:** the cause and the ship. The pictures see heat, not ships and not who started a fire.
- **What could tell more:** the next clear Sentinel-2 pass (`s2-look` at 26.30 N, 56.27 E), probably on
  28 Nov.

## What it shows about the rules (not changed)

- **The defect.** In open water, one earlier fire at a spot blocks a new NASA night detection there
  for the rest of the month.
- **Why it needs care.** The same block is also what stops a lingering ship from being posted every day
  as a new fire. The fix belongs to the identity work (`docs/plans/sea_fire_identity.md`):
  - in open water, use the open-water measure of usual heat;
  - say when a fire burned near the spot in the days before;
  - first silent, replayed and reviewed.
