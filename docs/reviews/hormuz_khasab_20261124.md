**Why it waited.** A pair of pictures is «a flare, not news» when the finder saw specks within
~5 km on 3 earlier nights (`sparkle_min_days`). Here those nights were the ship fire of 23-24 Nov
(confirmed, posted) and specks on 13, 17 and 19 Nov. The spot moved by about 1 km between pictures,
and only the 21:00 picture fell where just 2 earlier nights counted. The pair was released then:
the closer look was asked at 21:08:36, the video was made at 21:09:53 and the post went out at 21:10:09.

**Change.** The same rule as `engine.usual_heat` (open water, 23 Nov): in open water, with no
facility or platform within 5 km (ports aside) and outside every field of platforms NASA's long-term
map knows (`patterns.platform_area`), a flare is a speck on most of the 14 nights kept: 7.
Among platforms the plain 3 stays (Zakum, Zirku). `seawatch.sparkle_nights` serves the finder's
pairs and the first sightings (`preliminary._usual`).

**Replayed on this night.** The pair of 19:30 UTC (22:30 Baghdad) is news under the new rule. Read
about 8 minutes after its picture, with the closer look's video a minute or two later, the post would
have gone out at about 22:40 Baghdad, if that look showed the fire. That is about 1.5 hours before
the other channel.
